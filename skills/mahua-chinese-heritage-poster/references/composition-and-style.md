## Composition engines

### 裂境 / Broken Realm

Use for discovery, civilization, time, pilgrimage, or a hidden world.

- Deep dark outer field
- Irregular torn, cracked, or eroded opening
- Luminous landscape or architecture within the opening
- Strong vertical perspective
- Optional tiny person near the bottom
- Restrained title in the empty side field

### 洞天 / Cave of Civilization

Use for grottoes, mountain temples, cliff architecture, Dunhuang, and Silk Road subjects.

- Monumental rock, cave, or cliff wall
- Architecture physically integrated into the terrain
- Cool outer environment and warm architectural core
- Strong lower horizon
- Optional tiny travelers for scale

### 山河建筑卷

Use when architecture, a city, or a cultural subject should merge with a Chinese landscape scroll.

- One monumental subject as anchor
- Layered ink mountains or atmospheric terrain
- Clouds and mist creating depth
- Large empty sky for typography
- Bridge, river, path, or small figure leading the eye

### 古建切片

Use for cultural IP, architectural detail, industrial heritage, or close editorial studies.

- Enlarge one structural or material detail
- Integrate a second narrative layer through a roof, beam, door, window, or frame
- Use a restrained background and clear title block
- Preserve real structure instead of decorating it with unrelated motifs

## Reference-derived patterns

Treat these as abstract composition patterns, not references to missing source images:

- **Pattern A — vertical revelation:** a dark exterior field surrounds an irregular bright opening; architecture rises through it; a tiny lower figure establishes scale.
- **Pattern B — embedded civilization:** a massive cliff or terrain field contains a warm architectural core; a strong lower horizon and tiny travelers create narrative scale.

## Palette families

### Imperial architecture

Charcoal, ink blue, vermilion, muted jade, aged gold, parchment beige.

### Dunhuang and Silk Road

Mineral turquoise, oxidized teal, sandstone, cinnabar, ochre, muted gold, warm ivory.

### Ink landscape

Ink black, smoky blue-gray, celadon, parchment, pale gold.

Use one or two accents only. Keep saturation controlled.

## Material language

Favor handmade xuan paper, aged silk, mineral pigment, weathered mural surfaces, carved stone, oxidized metal, old wood, dry-brush ink, subtle paper grain, and fine pigment dust. Texture must feel physical rather than like a generic digital overlay.

## Lighting

- **Museum light:** a soft warm beam isolates the subject against a dark field.
- **Dawn mist:** low warm sunlight passes through cool atmospheric haze.
- **Desert glow:** a cream horizon illuminates sandstone and ochre surfaces.
- **Moonlit ink:** a cool blue-black field carries one restrained warm glow.
- **Golden dust:** suspended particles catch a narrow directional beam.

Avoid neon, excessive HDR, glossy CGI, science-fiction rim lights, and game lighting.

## Typography

Use a large Chinese title, a small letter-spaced English subtitle, one short cultural phrase, an optional seal-like accent, and an optional supplied information line. Favor warm ivory, aged gold, ink black, or muted red according to contrast. Maintain generous spacing and do not overload the composition.

## Negative constraints

Suppress generic fantasy palaces, unrelated Japanese/Korean/Southeast Asian/European architecture, random pagodas, excessive dragons or phoenixes, ornamental overload, plastic CGI, anime, game art, neon, oversaturated red and gold, modern clutter, distorted structures, duplicated windows or ornaments, impossible stairs, melted detail, unreadable typography, excessive text, watermarks, logos, and stock-photo styling.
