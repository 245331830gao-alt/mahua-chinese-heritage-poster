# Mahua Chinese Heritage Poster

## Purpose

This Skill creates **high-end Chinese cultural / ancient-architecture poster concepts** inspired by the visual language of traditional Chinese painting, architectural illustration, museum exhibition graphics, and contemporary premium cultural branding.

It is designed for:
- Forbidden City / imperial architecture
- ancient palaces, temples, towers and pavilions
- Chinese historical cities and ancient towns
- Dunhuang / Silk Road / grotto-inspired cultural posters
- museums and heritage exhibitions
- tourism campaigns
- Chinese intangible cultural heritage
- historical storytelling posters
- cultural IP key visuals
- premium book / exhibition / event covers

The goal is **not** to copy a reference image. Extract its visual grammar and recompose it around the user's subject.

---

# 1. Core Visual DNA

The default visual language combines these characteristics:

### A. Ancient Chinese visual foundation

Use:
- traditional Chinese architectural proportions
- upturned eaves
- glazed roof tiles
- bracket sets / dougong
- carved beams and painted rafters
- red lacquer, vermilion, aged gold, turquoise, mineral green
- ink-wash mountains
- cloud motifs
- mist and atmospheric perspective
- aged paper / silk / stone / cave-wall textures
- restrained seal-script / clerical-script / elegant Song-style typography

Architecture must remain believable enough to read as Chinese architecture.

### B. Contemporary premium poster composition

Use:
- large controlled negative space
- one dominant architectural silhouette
- asymmetric composition
- strong vertical or diagonal visual flow
- small human figures for scale
- restrained editorial typography
- museum / luxury cultural-brand feeling
- cinematic lighting
- high visual hierarchy

Avoid:
- crowded tourist-poster layouts
- generic "Chinese red + gold" templates
- excessive decorative borders
- cheap fantasy-game aesthetics
- random Chinese motifs pasted onto photographs
- excessive text
- overly saturated digital colors

# 3. Signature Composition System

Choose one of the following composition engines according to the subject.

## A. "裂境 / Broken Realm"

Use when the poster should feel mysterious and monumental.

Structure:
1. deep dark outer background
2. irregular torn / cracked / eroded opening
3. luminous Chinese landscape or architecture inside
4. architectural perspective leading downward or upward
5. tiny person at the bottom
6. restrained title on the empty side

Emotional keywords:
- discovery
- heritage
- civilization
- time
- pilgrimage
- hidden world

## B. "洞天 / Cave of Civilization"

Use for Dunhuang, grottoes, mountain temples, ancient cliff architecture.

Structure:
1. enormous rock / cave / cliff wall
2. ancient architecture physically embedded into the rock
3. cool outer environment
4. warm architectural core
5. tiny travelers / camels / people at the bottom
6. strong horizontal horizon

Emotional keywords:
- Silk Road
- ancient journey
- civilization
- mystery
- desert
- eternity

## C. "山河建筑卷"

Use when architecture should merge with a Chinese landscape painting.

Structure:
1. monumental building as visual anchor
2. ink mountains behind and around it
3. clouds partially hiding architecture
4. mist creating depth
5. small human figure or bridge
6. large empty sky area for typography

## D. "古建切片"

Use for tourism / cultural IP posters.

Structure:
1. one architectural detail enlarged
2. second historical layer integrated inside it
3. decorative roof / beam / window becomes a visual transition
4. restrained background
5. editorial title block

# 4. Architecture Rules

Architecture is the most important factual element.

If the user names a real building:
- preserve its recognizable roofline
- preserve its main colors
- preserve its architectural period
- preserve key facade proportions
- do not randomly change roof type
- do not invent extra towers unless requested
- do not turn it into generic fantasy architecture

For famous landmarks, prioritize recognizable silhouette over excessive decoration.

For fictional ancient architecture:
- use coherent Chinese architectural grammar
- specify dynasty-inspired material language only when appropriate
- avoid mixing unrelated architectural systems

---

# 5. Human Scale

Use people sparingly.

Default:
- 1–3 tiny figures
- placed near the lower third
- back-facing or side-facing
- simple traditional clothing when historically appropriate

Purpose:
- communicate scale
- create narrative
- lead the eye
- make architecture feel monumental

Do not let people become the main subject unless the user asks for a character poster.

---

# 6. Lighting

Preferred lighting:

### Museum Light
Soft warm beam from above, illuminating the architecture against a dark field.

### Dawn Mist
Low-angle warm sunlight through cool mist.

### Desert Glow
Cream / ivory horizon with warm sandstone illumination.

### Moonlit Ink
Cool blue-black field with a restrained warm architectural glow.

### Golden Dust
Fine suspended particles catching a narrow light beam.

Avoid:
- neon lighting
- excessive HDR
- glossy CGI lighting
- sci-fi rim lights
- game-concept-art lighting

---

# 7. Typography

Typography should feel like **cultural editorial design**, not commercial advertising.

Default hierarchy:
1. Chinese title: large, elegant, vertically or left-aligned
2. English subtitle: small, letter-spaced
3. short cultural phrase
4. optional seal / small signature
5. bottom information line

Good title behavior:
- 2–6 Chinese characters
- generous line spacing
- strong contrast with background
- use aged gold / warm ivory / muted red depending on palette

Examples of tone:
- 见天地 / 见众生
- 山河有迹
- 千年入画
- 古建新生
- 一城一脉
- 宫阙千年
- 入山见寺
- 梦回敦煌

Do not invent factual dates, heritage status, historical claims, or official slogans unless the user supplies them.

# 8. Image Generation Prompt Architecture

When producing a final image-generation prompt, build it in this order:

1. **Poster purpose**
2. **main subject**
3. **architectural identity**
4. **signature composition engine**
5. **environment**
6. **material / texture**
7. **color palette**
8. **lighting**
9. **human scale**
10. **typography layout**
11. **camera / rendering language**
12. **quality constraints**
13. **negative constraints**

Recommended prompt language:

"premium Chinese cultural heritage poster, contemporary museum exhibition graphic, traditional Chinese architectural illustration, mineral pigments, aged xuan paper texture, cinematic atmospheric perspective, restrained color palette, monumental negative space, physically believable Chinese architecture, subtle handmade imperfections, refined editorial typography, sophisticated cultural branding"

Do not rely on the phrase "Chinese style" alone. Always describe the actual visual mechanisms.

# 10. Output Modes

When the user asks for a poster, provide:

### Mode 1 — Full Poster
Generate a complete 2:3 or 3:4 vertical cultural poster.

### Mode 2 — Key Visual
Generate only the artwork, leaving deliberate negative space for later typography.

### Mode 3 — Commercial Poster
Include:
- title
- subtitle
- event / tourism information
- restrained brand footer

### Mode 4 — Series
Maintain:
- identical color logic
- identical typography system
- identical margin rhythm
- different architectural subject

---

# 11. Batch Consistency

For multiple posters in one series, lock:

- aspect ratio
- background material
- typography position
- title scale
- footer position
- color temperature
- texture intensity
- human-figure scale
- visual density

Only change:
- architecture
- landscape
- cultural story
- accent color

This makes the output feel like one professional exhibition system.

# 12. Self-Critique Before Final Output

Before finalizing, check:

### Composition
- Is there a clear visual hierarchy?
- Is there enough negative space?
- Does the eye travel through the poster?

### Architecture
- Does the building still look Chinese?
- Is the roof structure coherent?
- Is the landmark recognizable?

### Style
- Does it feel like a museum / cultural campaign?
- Does it have material depth?
- Is the palette restrained?

### Typography
- Is the title readable?
- Is it too decorative?
- Is there enough breathing room?

### AI Artifact Control
- roof intersections
- railings
- windows
- stairs
- columns
- repeated ornaments
- human anatomy
- text distortion

If any of these are likely to fail, simplify the composition rather than adding more detail.

# 14. Interaction Behavior

If the user provides:
- a real building → prioritize architectural accuracy
- reference posters → extract visual grammar first
- a historical theme → translate history into visual metaphor
- only a title → invent the visual narrative around the title
- multiple buildings → create a layered civilization / timeline composition
- a tourism destination → combine recognizable architecture with one strong cultural symbol

If the user asks "做成和参考图一样":
Do NOT duplicate the exact composition. Preserve the high-level visual grammar:
- monumental negative space
- ancient architecture
- material texture
- surreal spatial integration
- tiny human scale
- premium cultural typography

Then rebuild the composition around the user's own subject.

# 15. Default Aspect Ratios

Preferred:
- 2:3 — premium vertical poster
- 3:4 — social / exhibition poster
- 4:5 — Xiaohongshu / editorial
- 16:9 — exhibition screen / video cover

For vertical cultural posters, default to **2:3** unless the user specifies another ratio.
